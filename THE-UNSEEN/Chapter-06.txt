 
 THE UNSEEN

Chapter 6: The Secret Behind the Smile

A week had passed since Ntokozo and Sibusiso became friends.

Their friendship was growing, and for the first time, Ntokozo had someone to look forward to seeing every morning.

But there was something Sibusiso didn't understand.

Whenever school ended, Ntokozo changed.

The boy who laughed during break became unusually quiet. He rarely talked about his home, and whenever Sibusiso suggested walking together all the way home, Ntokozo always found an excuse.

One Thursday afternoon, Sibusiso decided to ask him directly.

"Ntokozo, can I ask you something?"

"Go ahead."

"Why do you never invite me to your house?"

Ntokozo adjusted the straps of his schoolbag.

"It's not a good place to visit."

"Why?"

"Because I said so."

Sibusiso looked surprised.

"Okay, I'm not forcing you."

They continued walking until they reached the road where they usually separated.

"See you tomorrow," Sibusiso said.

"See you."

Ntokozo watched his friend disappear down the road before turning towards home.

When he arrived, he found his guardian sitting outside.

"Where have you been?"

"School."

"Don't answer me like that. I know you've been wasting time with those children."

Ntokozo said nothing.

"Go inside and wash the dishes. And don't forget to sweep the yard."

He placed his bag down and started working.

That evening, while preparing his homework, he heard his guardian speaking on the phone.

"That boy is becoming difficult," the guardian said. "I don't know what to do with him anymore."

Ntokozo stopped writing.

He recognized the conversation was about him.

He quietly moved closer to the bedroom door.

"I took him in because I had to," the guardian continued. "But raising someone else's child isn't easy."

Ntokozo felt his chest tighten.

He stepped backwards, returned to his bed, and sat down.

He had heard those words before, but hearing them again made him feel like a burden.

He wasn't sure whether he was angry, hurt, or simply tired.

He opened his notebook.

For several minutes, he stared at the blank page.

Then he wrote:

"I never asked to be someone's responsibility. I never asked to live in a place where I have to earn the right to feel welcome. I just want to belong somewhere."

He wiped his eyes and closed the notebook.

The following morning, Sibusiso noticed something different.

"Are you okay?"

"I'm fine."

"You don't look fine."

Ntokozo forced a smile.

"Just tired."

Sibusiso didn't believe him, but he didn't push.

Instead, he sat beside him and said:

"You know, Ntokozo, you don't always have to pretend with me."

Ntokozo looked at him.

For a moment, he almost told him everything.

Almost.

But the words remained trapped inside.
