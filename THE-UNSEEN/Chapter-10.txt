THE UNSEEN

 Chapter 10: The Wound Nobody Could See

The rain had stopped, but the morning remained cold.

Ntokozo arrived at school earlier than usual. He placed his bag beside his desk and took out the notebook Mr. Dlamini had given him.

He opened it and read some of the things he had written over the past few days.

He smiled at a few sentences.

Then he reached a page he had written the previous night.

"I have a friend now, but I still don't know how to be happy without being afraid."

He closed the notebook when Sibusiso entered the classroom.

"Morning, my brother!"

Ntokozo smiled.

"Morning."

"You're looking better today."

"Maybe because I slept well."

"Good. We have a mathematics test today. I hope you're ready."

Ntokozo laughed.

"You're the one who should be worried."

Their laughter attracted the attention of other students.

For once, Ntokozo didn't mind being noticed.

However, the happiness didn't last long.

During the afternoon lesson, the teacher asked everyone to write a short composition titled My Family.

Some students immediately began writing about their parents, siblings, and family holidays.

Ntokozo stared at the empty page.

He held his pen but couldn't write a single sentence.

What was he supposed to say?

That he lived with a guardian?

That he didn't remember much about the people who were supposed to protect him?

That his home sometimes felt like a place where he was merely tolerated?

The teacher walked around the classroom, checking everyone's progress.

When he reached Ntokozo's desk, he noticed the empty page.

"Is everything alright?"

Ntokozo nodded.

"I'm just thinking, sir."

The teacher moved on.

At break time, Ntokozo sat quietly beneath the tree.

Sibusiso approached him.

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing."

"Ntokozo, you always say nothing."

Ntokozo looked at him.

"Do you ever wish your life was different?"

Sibusiso sat beside him.

"Sometimes. Why?"

Ntokozo took a deep breath.

"Because I don't really know what it feels like to have a family that wants you."

Sibusiso became quiet.

Ntokozo continued.

"I live with my guardian, but sometimes I feel like I'm just another responsibility. I don't think anyone understands how that feels."

Sibusiso didn't interrupt.

For the first time, Ntokozo had said something he had been hiding for years.

"I'm sorry, Ntokozo," Sibusiso finally said.

Ntokozo looked away.

"Don't be. You didn't do anything."

Sibusiso placed a hand on his shoulder.

"You know something? You don't have to face everything alone."

Ntokozo nodded.

But deep inside, he knew that some wounds were difficult to explain, even to the people who cared.

That evening, he returned home and went straight to his bedroom.

He opened his notebook and wrote:

"Today, I wanted to write about my family, but I didn't know where to begin. How do you describe a home where you don't always feel wanted? How do you explain loneliness to someone who has never experienced it?"

He stopped writing.

Then he added one final sentence:

"Maybe the deepest wounds are the ones nobody can see."

He closed the notebook.

For the first time, he had shared a piece of his pain with someone.

And although nothing had changed at home, something had changed inside him.
