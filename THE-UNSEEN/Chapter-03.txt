

 Chapter 3: The Empty Seat

Monday morning arrived with the familiar sound of schoolchildren filling the streets.

Ntokozo woke up early, prepared his uniform, washed his face, and packed his books.

Before leaving, he looked at himself in the small mirror hanging beside his bedroom door.

"Maybe today will be different," he whispered.

He didn't know why he kept telling himself that. Perhaps it was hope. Perhaps he simply needed something to believe in.

At school, he entered the classroom and went straight to his desk.

A group of boys near the window were discussing a football match they had watched over the weekend.

"I told you Barcelona would win!" one of them shouted.

Everyone laughed.

Ntokozo smiled. He loved football too, but nobody knew that. Nobody had ever asked him which team he supported.

During the lesson, the teacher announced a group assignment.

"Choose your groups of four. You will work together until Friday."

Within seconds, the classroom became noisy.

Chairs moved. Students called their friends. Groups formed almost immediately.

Ntokozo remained seated.

He looked around, hoping someone would invite him.

One group had four members.

Another had five.

A few students were still searching for partners, but nobody approached him.

Finally, the teacher noticed.

"Ntokozo, why are you sitting alone?"

He stood up.

"I don't have a group, sir."

The teacher looked around and pointed towards a group near the front.

"Join them."

Ntokozo picked up his books and walked towards them.

One of the boys sighed.

"Sir, we're already four."

"Then make room for one more," the teacher replied.

Ntokozo sat down without saying anything.

He could feel that he wasn't wanted.

For the rest of the lesson, he barely spoke. He simply wrote down the answers and waited for the bell.

When school ended, he stayed behind for a few minutes.

He looked at the empty desks.

Funny how a classroom could be full of people and still make someone feel completely alone.

That evening, he opened his notebook again.

He wrote:

"Today, I learned something. Being surrounded by people doesn't mean you have company. Sometimes, the loneliest place in the world is a classroom full of students."

He closed the notebook.

But this time, before hiding it beneath his mattress, he added another sentence:

"I wish someone would choose me without being told to."
