

THE UNSEEN

Chapter 4: The Stranger Who Noticed

Tuesday morning began like every other school day.

Ntokozo arrived early, placed his schoolbag beside his desk, and sat quietly while the classroom slowly filled with students.

He took out his mathematics book and began solving a few exercises.

A few minutes later, the classroom door opened.

A new student walked in.

He was slightly taller than most boys in the class, wore a clean uniform, and carried a black backpack.

"Good morning, everyone," the teacher announced. "We have a new student joining us today. His name is Sibusiso."

The class responded with a few greetings.

"Find an empty seat, Sibusiso."

There were several empty desks, but the only one near the window was beside Ntokozo.

Sibusiso walked towards it.

"Is anyone sitting here?" he asked.

Ntokozo looked up.

"No."

"Mind if I sit?"

Ntokozo shook his head.

Sibusiso placed his bag down and sat beside him.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Sibusiso noticed the mathematics exercises on Ntokozo's desk.

"You've already started?"

Ntokozo nodded.

"You're fast."

"Not really," Ntokozo replied quietly.

Sibusiso smiled.

"I'm Sibusiso, by the way."

"Ntokozo."

"I know. The teacher mentioned your name."

Ntokozo looked down at his book.

It was a small conversation, nothing extraordinary. But something about it felt different.

For once, someone had spoken to him without being instructed to do so.

During break time, Ntokozo prepared to leave the classroom as usual.

"Hey, Ntokozo!"

He turned around.

Sibusiso was standing near the door.

"Where are you going?"

"Outside."

"Mind if I come with you?"

Ntokozo hesitated.

"Sure."

They walked towards the school grounds together.

They talked about football, their favorite subjects, and the things they enjoyed doing after school.

For the first time in a long while, Ntokozo laughed without pretending.

But as they returned to class, Sibusiso asked something that caught him off guard.

"Why do you always sit alone?"

Ntokozo stopped walking.

He didn't know how to answer.

After a few seconds, he simply said:

"I'm used to it."

Sibusiso looked at him but didn't push the question.

"Well, you don't have to sit alone anymore."

Ntokozo smiled.

He tried not to show how much those words meant to him.

That afternoon, on his way home, he kept remembering their conversation.

When he reached his bedroom, he opened his notebook and wrote:

"Today, someone called my name. Not because the teacher told him to. Not because he needed something from me. He simply wanted to talk."

He paused, then added:

"Maybe I am not as invisible as I thought."

For the first time in months, Ntokozo went to sleep smiling.

But he didn't know that friendship could sometimes bring questions he wasn't ready to answer.
