

 THE UNSEEN
 Chapter 9: The Price of Friendship

Monday morning arrived with heavy clouds hanging over the school.

Ntokozo walked through the school gate, carrying the notebook Mr. Dlamini had given him. He had spent most of Sunday writing in it. For the first time, he had somewhere to put all the thoughts he had kept inside for years.

As he approached his classroom, he heard someone calling his name.

"Ntokozo!"

It was Sibusiso.

"Where have you been? I've been waiting for you."

Ntokozo smiled.

"Sorry. I had some things to do at home."

They entered the classroom together.

But something was different.

A group of boys sitting near the back kept looking at them and whispering. One of them, a boy named Thabo, seemed particularly interested in their friendship.

During break, Thabo approached Sibusiso.

"Why are you always with that boy?"

Sibusiso looked confused.

"Which boy?"

"Ntokozo."

"Because he's my friend."

Thabo laughed.

"Seriously? You could have better friends than him."

Sibusiso's expression changed.

"What's wrong with him?"

"Nothing. He's just strange. He barely talks, and nobody even knows anything about him."

Sibusiso shook his head.

"You don't have to understand everyone to treat them with respect."

Thabo walked away, laughing.

Ntokozo had heard everything.

He stood a few metres away, pretending to be busy arranging his books.

The words hurt, but what hurt more was the fear that Sibusiso might eventually agree with them.

That afternoon, Ntokozo decided to walk home alone.

Sibusiso caught up with him near the school gate.

"Why didn't you wait for me?"

"I wanted to walk alone."

"Did I do something?"

"No."

Sibusiso stopped walking.

"Then why are you avoiding me?"

Ntokozo looked at the ground.

"I heard what Thabo said."

"So?"

"Maybe he's right. Maybe you should find better friends."

Sibusiso stared at him for a moment.

"Ntokozo, I don't choose my friends because other people approve of them. I choose them because of who they are."

Ntokozo said nothing.

Sibusiso continued:

"And I like having you around. Don't let someone else's opinion decide what you think about yourself."

They continued walking.

For the first time, Ntokozo understood that friendship wasn't only about laughing together. Sometimes it meant standing beside someone when the world tried to push them away.

That evening, he opened his notebook.

"Today, I almost pushed away the only friend I have because I was afraid he would eventually leave me. Maybe my greatest enemy isn't loneliness. Maybe it's the fear that happiness never lasts."

He closed the notebook and placed it beneath his pillow.

Outside, the rain began to fall.

And somewhere in the distance, a new problem was quietly approaching.
