

We're continuing THE UNSEEN, featuring Ntokozo, aged 14, who lives with a guardian.

We've completed Chapter 1: The Boy Nobody Noticed.

For Chapter 2, I want to explore what happens when Ntokozo gets home. We'll see how his guardian treats him, what his home life is like, and why he keeps so much inside.

# Chapter 2: A House Without Warmth

The journey home was always the same for Ntokozo.

He walked along the dusty road, passing children playing football, mothers selling vegetables, and friends walking together while sharing stories about their day.

He watched them quietly.

Sometimes he wondered what it felt like to have someone waiting for you, someone who genuinely looked forward to seeing you.

When he finally reached home, he pushed open the gate and entered the small yard.

"I'm back," he announced.

There was no response.

His guardian was inside the house, watching television.

"You're late," the guardian said without looking at him.

"Sorry. We had some extra work at school."

"Did you finish your chores yesterday?"

Ntokozo lowered his eyes.

"I'll do them now."

He placed his schoolbag in the corner and started sweeping the yard. Nobody asked how his day had gone. Nobody asked whether he had eaten lunch or whether something had happened at school.

He had learned not to expect those questions.

Later that evening, he sat on the edge of his bed with his exercise book open.

He had homework to complete, but his mind was somewhere else.

He remembered the laughter at school.

He remembered the empty seat beside him.

He remembered how easily everyone seemed to find someone to talk to.

Then he opened his secret notebook.

He wrote:

"I live in a house, but sometimes it doesn't feel like home. I go to school, but sometimes it doesn't feel like I belong there either. I wonder where someone like me is supposed to belong."

He stopped writing.

Outside, the wind moved gently through the trees.

Ntokozo closed his notebook and placed it beneath his mattress.

Tomorrow would probably be another ordinary day.

Or at least, that's what he thought.
